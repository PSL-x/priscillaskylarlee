## Situation
Multiple teams across SAP's Data & Analytics portfolio were building similar elements independently – accumulating design debt, slowing product development, and creating inconsistent experiences.

Key challenges included:
- No shared library for Data & Analytics custom elements
- Ongoing migration to SAP Fiori across products
- Inconsistent patterns and component implementation across different analytics products
- Designers and developers rebuilding the same elements, slightly differently each time
- No accessibility standards or documentation

![Component library visualization](/images/work/dna-components.svg)

## Impact
- DNA Web UI Kit adopted across our organization's product teams
- Design-to-development time reduced by 40%
- All 20+ custom elements meet WCAG 2.1 AA compliance
- Internal audits show measurable improvements in design consistency across the portfolio

![Adoption metrics and impact visualization](/images/work/dna-adoption.svg)

## A Note on AI

Building design systems now means navigating a new tension: AI tools can generate components, variations, and entire interfaces in seconds. This makes the question of *'what deserves to be systematic'* more critical than ever.

Our work isn't just about providing reusable elements — it's about identifying guidelines for when and where we need human intervention. We define:
- **What should be automated** (color token generation, responsive spacing)
- **What needs guardrails** (accessibility requirements, data visualization patterns)
- **What requires human judgment** (complex interaction patterns, ethical edge cases, human context of each element)

The system becomes an ethical framework: documentation that explains not just *how* to build, but *why* certain patterns exist and when folks should be deviating.


## Notes
- Design systems are living products — the hardest part isn't building elements, it's building a culture where teams contribute back and understand *why* standards matter.
- As AI accelerates production, design systems shift from "component libraries" to "frameworks for intentionality" — defining what deserves consistency and what needs human judgment.
- Documentation is critical; teams need guidance on when and how to use patterns, not just access to them. In an AI age, documentation becomes an ethical compass, not just a reference.
- Process, process, process. Checklists and review standards help the team lifecycle, and matter just as much as the elements themselves.
- The best systems make the right thing easy while preserving space for meaningful human decisions.
- There's always something new to learn, and that learning happens everywhere.
