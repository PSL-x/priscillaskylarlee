# Lisfranc

The Lisfranc joint is where the metatarsal bones in the forefoot meet the tarsal bones in the midfoot, held together by a set of ligaments most people don't know exist until one of them fails. The injury is frequently misdiagnosed as a sprain because it doesn't look dramatic on a regular X-ray. Mine wasn't a sprain.

In June 2025 I had surgery on my foot. Non-weight bearing until August, walking again in late August, with a triathlon already registered for September.

I did the triathlon anyway.

## What I lost

The obvious loss was mobility. For weeks I couldn't walk, couldn't exercise, couldn't do basic things for myself, and not just physically: it does something to your sense of self when you can't move through the world the way you're used to.

The less obvious loss was community. The injury meant retiring from dodgeball, a sport I'd played for years, and when you leave a sport you don't just lose the game. You lose the people, the schedule, the version of yourself that showed up every week.

## The triathlon

The race was September 6, 2025, about ten days after I was cleared to walk. My physio knew I was going. She wasn't particularly pleased.

I swam, I cycled, and then I walked the entire 10k run portion. The spectators didn't know; they were cheering at the side of the course, telling me to keep running, saying I was almost there. I knew I wasn't allowed to run. I smiled and kept walking.

I didn't finish last.

My physio had some thoughts about the 10k walk. She wasn't wrong. But I needed to finish that race more than I needed to be sensible about it, and I think that was the right call for where I was mentally.

## What I learnt

I travelled while non-weight bearing — to Germany, and to a work program in the US. Moving through the world on crutches, depending on other people, not being able to control what was possible on any given day. I hated it and I needed it.

The thing I wish someone had told me earlier: it's okay to let go of control. The growth that happens during the hard part isn't legible while you're in it, and you don't see what you're learning until later, sometimes much later.

I'm still in the later.

## Where I am now

Training for the T100 Vancouver, Olympic distance. My goal is to beat my time from last year, which should be achievable given that I walked the run. Emphasis on should. Bodies are unpredictable and I've learned not to assume.

Some things are still unresolved: the identity piece, the community piece, what my relationship to sport looks like now that it's shifted. I'm evaluating that slowly, and not everything has a clean conclusion yet.

## Notes

- Non-weight bearing is genuinely hard. Let people help you.
- The timeline is longer than you want it to be and shorter than you fear.
- Grief about losing a sport or a community is real. Name it.
- You will probably do something your physio isn't pleased about. Try to make it worth it.
