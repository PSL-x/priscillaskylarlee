# Building a Custom Keyboard

I'd used mechanical keyboards for years without ever building one. The builds looked involved and the community looked obsessive in a way that made me suspicious. Eventually I bought a 65% kit anyway.

## The build

Aluminum case, hot-swap PCB, 67 Cherry MX Browns, PBT keycaps in XDA profile. No soldering — hot-swap sockets let you press switches directly into the board. Assembly was about 3 hours, most of which was stabilizers.

## What I didn't expect

**The weight changes how it feels to type.** The aluminum case is heavy compared to every plastic keyboard I've used. It doesn't move on the desk. I didn't notice the sliding until it stopped.

**Stabilizers are where builds go wrong.** The large keys — spacebar, backspace, shift — use stabilizers to stay even when you press off-center. Out of the box, they rattle. Getting them right involves clipping the legs, lubing the housing and wire, and applying foam underneath. I watched four different tutorials and still got it wrong twice. The difference between a rattly stabilizer and a smooth one is significant enough to matter every single keystroke.

**XDA felt strange for about a day.** OEM profile has a sculpted row height — keys lower toward the center. XDA is flat across the whole board. The uniformity took adjustment. Now I prefer it.

## The problem with this hobby

Every component is a variable. Switches alone — linear, tactile, or clicky, dozens of options in each category, different spring weights and travel distances. Then keycap profiles. Then case materials and mounting styles. The people who end up with ten keyboards aren't confused; they're systematically exploring the space.

Next build is already planned: 75% layout, Boba U4T switches, gasket mount. Gasket mount isolates the plate from the case, which is supposed to make the typing feel softer. I want to find out if I can actually notice the difference.

Stabilizers first, though: get those right before anything else.
